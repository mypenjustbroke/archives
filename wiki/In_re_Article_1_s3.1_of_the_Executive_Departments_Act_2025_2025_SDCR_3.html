In re Article 1 s3.1 of the Executive Departments Act 2025 [2025] SDCR 3

Date 12th October 2025
Judges
  • Chief Judge Benbookworm
  • Justice Terak
  • Justice hmquestionable
Held
  • Article 1§3.1 of the Executive Departments Act 2025 is constitutional.
Ruling 2-1
Applicable precedent
  • The President is to execute the laws, not operate contrary to them.
    • See In re War and Peace Act 2024 [2024] SDSC 1 [27]
  • The President may act in the absence of legislation
  • Dissent: The President can give an additional department responsibilities already assigned by statute to another department, [22]

MAJORITY OPINION by Chief Judge Ben

(with Judge Terak agreeing)

Introduction

[1] The petitioner challenges the constitutionality of the Article 1§1.3 of the Executive Departments Act 2025, which reads: 

“§3.1 Non-statutory departments may not be assigned portfolios, powers, and duties already assigned to statutory departments.”

The petitioner primarily cites Article 7§2 of the Constitution: 

“Executive Officers shall be the heads of executive bodies, as created by legislation or by decree of the President. They shall wield executive authority over the department they lead, behind only the authority of the President and the Vice-President”

[2] The respondent argues that the Legislature is permitted to impose “reasonable restrictions” on the activities of the Executive, so far as they do not enter the realms of direct or indirect cannibalization. In support of this point, the respondent cites Article 5§4 of the Constitution: 

“The President shall have primary authority over the organization and management of the core territories of SimDemocracy, in the absence of specific legislation otherwise.”

They argue that this section allows the Senate to determine “the organization and management of the core territories through legislation, which is normally an executive duty”.

Doctrine of Cannibalization

[3] The doctrine of cannibalization arises from In re War and Peace Act 2024 [2024] SDSC 1, which reads in [27]: 

“The Senate can not make laws which give it powers in contradiction of the Constitution, because such an act would by its nature be unconstitutional and void. [...][T]he Senate is allowed to make a law which institutes a new department, but it is not allowed to make a law which states that the Senate is to run this new department. [...] Simply put, this is because the Senate has no executive authority under the Constitution [...] just as the President has no legislative authority. Utilizing their existing authority to try to cannibalize the other’s authority is therefore unconstitutional.”

Constitutionality

[4] However, the petitioner fundamentally misunderstands the meaning of the constitution. The Senate is permitted to create executive bodies, and as such the law is constitutional. The meaning of executive power is that the president must enforce the law, not violate by modifying legislation. While the president may also create executive bodies, to divest validly legislated entities of their statutory assignments validly legislated entities violates the separation of powers.

[5] Even if it were considered to be a restriction on executive power, the Senate’s ability to create executive bodies is explicitly permitted by the Constitution. The president has no power to invalidate legislation, whatsoever. Moving legislated portfolios from one department to another represents unilateral modification of the law.

[6] Executive officers must be confirmed by the Senate. If the president could attempt to reassign legislated matters to an entity of their own creation (headed by someone other than a full executive officer), such would be an endrun around that constitutional requirement.

[7] Article 1§3.1 of the Executive Departments Act 2025 is upheld as constitutional.

MAJORITY OPINION by Judge Terak

(with Chief Judge Benbookworm agreeing)

On Art. 5§4 Constitution

[8] Art. 5§4 Constitution establishes that the President may create and organize the core territories of SimDem and how they are administered, unless the Senate has created specific legislation. This is a fall back clause, closing any open holes in the legislation, because in case the Senate has made no rule regarding something and it concerns something vitally important, the president can make a rule through executive decree (as they often do through executive orders), until the Senate has closed that gap. For example, if need for a public body administering roles arises, the President could make a rule on how such a conduct should be administered until the Senate makes a statutory rule. For this specific example the Senate has already made a rule but the example still stands.

[9] If the Senate has made a law regarding the structure of the core territories of SimDemocracy, the administrative capabilities of the President are void for that area and they have to obey the laws enacted by the Senate, as far as these are constitutional.

On executive bodies and departments

[10] The state, as a legal entity, can not act by itself. It requires humans to actually act. These humans act not as themselves but as parts of the legal entity in wielding constitutional and statutory powers. They are bodies of the legal entity of the state.

[11] Under Art. 5§1 and Art. 6§1 Constitution, the President and Vice President are the first and second executive body respectively. This establishes the principle of splitting up the executive power between different public bodies but also creates clear links between these bodies, chaining them to the President as the primary body, thus making the other bodies subservient. This is further backed up by establishing the Senate as the sole legislative body in Art. 1 §1 Constitution, thus investing the entire legislative power within it, and the courts as a whole as the judicial body, invested with the judicial power. The separation of powers is clearly executed by splitting it into different bodies that can interact with each other but not intersect.

[12] The Constitution knows other executive bodies and enables bodies to not be bound to the execution of power in the state. Under Art. 7§1, the Cabinet is an advisory body to the President. The same paragraph specifies that this executive body may be changed by legislative action. The executive bodies, as long as the constitution is not violated, thus may be changed by the Senate where the constitution provides for it.

[13] Art. 7§2 establishes that each executive officer is in charge of their own executive bodies. These bodies may be created by the President or by legislative action. We established already that legislative power is vested solely in the Senate, thus when the Senate has enacted a law concerning an executive body, the president is not at liberty to violate that law. The power of the President to create bodies has to be seen in analogy to Art. 5§4 as described above. The power to shape the core territories rest with the President, as long as the Senate has not made their own decision. If they have made a decision though, they need to follow that decision.

[14] As described above, the executive bodies are headed by executive officers. These bodies, in practice are often the Departments. Departments wield the power bestowed upon them.

[15] Who now gets to decide the make-up of these departments. Art. 7§4.2 holds the answer. If a department has been created through executive decision by the President by delegating some of their powers upon a new department, they get to shape the amount of power and discretion they award to their new Department. If a new Department has been created through legislation though, they are not at liberty to shape it in the same way, given they had no say in its creation. The original frame of ability and discretion and authority are up to the power that creates a body. If a body has been created by the legislature, it gets to decide those factors. If they are creating an executive body to wield executive authority though, it is placed under the president who then can shape the conduct of that body, within the constitutional and statutory framework through their own power.

[16] If the President were to create a double of a statutory executive body to increase their own influence over that body, beyond the influence they already wield under Art. 7 §3ff. Constitution, by reducing the authority or discretion or power of a department, would run afoul of the legislative text that created that department, thus the president would assume the power to legislate as they would reduce the legislative text by their own power. A power the office of the president does not hold under the constitution. For these reasons I concur in upholding the constitutionality of the law before this court.

DISSENTING OPINION by Judge hmquestionable

Introduction

[17] I disagree that [Article 1§3.1 of the Executive Departments Act is constitutional.

Difference between the Constitution at the time of In re War and Peace and now

[18] There is a substantial difference between the Constitution as it existed during War and Peace, and as it exists now. For instance, nothing in the Constitution permitted the establishment of a cabinet by law.

[19] This major difference in the constitution is very important. Under the Constitution, the Senate can introduce checks on the ability of the President to make use of executive power, provided that such checks did not completely remove the power from the President. This was because nothing was specifically written on the Cabinet, and as such the usual test for cannibalization applied.

[20] However, the situation is different at this point. The Constitution reads: “§2. Executive Officers shall be the heads of executive bodies, as created by legislation or by decree of the President.” (emphasis added).

[21] Thus, I do not think there is anything related to cannibalization to be brought up here. Executive bodies can be created “by legislation” or “by decree of the President”. Saying that the President cannot create executive bodies by decree of the President under certain circumstances is unconstitutional, as the text here contains nothing allowing the Senate to restrict the ability of the President to create executive bodies.

[22] This is not to say that the President can unilaterally switch portfolios around within the departments of the Executive. Rather, the President can assign a portfolio to a certain department, but it can also be assigned by the Senate, in which case there would be multiple departments with the same portfolio.

[23] I think it is useful to draw upon the words of Halfcat CJ (as he then was) in War and Peace: “Something is either unconstitutional or not, it does not matter if it impacts 0.01% of an authority or 100% of it, its constitutionality remains the same.”.
