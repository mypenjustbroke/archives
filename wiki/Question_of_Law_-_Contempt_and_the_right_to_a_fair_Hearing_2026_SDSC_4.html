Question of Law - Contempt and the Right to a Fair Hearing 2026 SDSC 4

Date of judgment 16 April 2026
Justices
  • Chief Justice Ed
  • Justice TheLittleSparty
  • Justice IvyCactus
  • Justice Terak
Held Art. 21 Subdivision 1 Subsection 2 Criminal Code 2020 can not be used to bring a charge against a party's attorney.
Ruling 4-0
Applicable precedent
  • Time limits for proceedings according to Art. 30 § 5 CPA only begin to start after the Court accepted to hear the case
  • “Society” is not a defined group within SimDemocracy and is not able to hold rights separate to and independent of the rights of individuals
  • Affirms: In re Penalties and Tickets Act [2025] SDSC 17
  • The correct charge against an inactive attorney would be Art. 21 Subdivision 1 Subsection 3 Criminal Code.

MAJORITY OPINION by Justice Terak

(with Justice TheLittleSparty agreeing, and Chief Justice Ed and Justice Ivy Cactus concurring)

Introduction

[1] The Court of Review submitted a question of law before the Supreme Court as a qualified question under Art. 30 § 1.2 CPA. The question concerns the relationship between a judge's ability to hold a person in contempt of court for not participating in the trial proceedings and the accused's rights to a fair hearing. The Court posed in its question a right of the society to have a functioning Justice system and asked about the conflict that arises out of that right with a potential inability to prosecute someone for contempt for non-participation.

[2] The Supreme Court voted to hear the Petition for a Question of Law.

On the procedural framework and the statutory nature of a Question of Law to the Supreme Court

[3] The Court of Reviews petition alone raises important questions as to the procedural nature of the question and the provisions of Art. 30 § 1.2 CPA.

[4] Art. 30 § 1.2 CPA stipulates that a court may pause a proceeding and pose a question of law, relevant to an ongoing case, to a higher court of law, if they believe the underlying legal basis is unconstitutional. This question has to be answered within 10 days by that higher court. In the case of the Supreme Court, the law cites Art. 11 § 5.1 Constitution as legal authority for the time limit.

[5] Art. 11 § 5 Constitution provides that the Supreme Court may regulate its own proceedings without outside influences. Art. 11 § 5.1 Constitution mandates an exception where the Senate by passing a law may mandate specific time frames for proceedings to be followed for matters before the court. The question thus becomes, when is a matter “before the court”. The rest of the Paragraph gives further inside and thus is worth replicating in full here: “That matters before the Court be heard and disposed of within a certain time-frame as specified by law;” The Senate may thus only regulate time limits for matters already before the Supreme Court that are decided to be heard and ruled upon. The Constitutional Provision does not unfold any effect before the Court has accepted to hear a matter. Any statutory provision that regulates the Supreme Court's Conduct before the Court has voted to accept a petition thus is in violation of the Constitution and void.

[6] The CPA directly points to the Constitutional Provision of the Art. 11 § 5.1 to understand how the time limit imposed works in relation to the Supreme Court. The Provision in Art. 29 CPA has to be understood therefore as meaning the time limit applies to the Supreme Court only, after the Court has voted to hear a matter.

Introduction into the Court of Review’s Question

[7] The Question posed by the Court is meant to discern the interaction between the right to a fair hearing under Art. 21 § 1 Constitution and a Court's Ability to hold a person in Contempt under Art. 21 Subdivision 1 Subsection 2 § 1 (b) Criminal Code, for matters where the contempt arises from an attorney's failure to participate in a judicial proceeding, resulting in them becoming held in contempt and contempt proceedings being started against them by the court.

[8] The Court of Review further cites the provisions for Art. 21 §§ 3, 4 Constitution as applicable to contempt proceedings. The Court argues that the current procedure as laid out in the Criminal Court would essentially allow the Court to hold a person in contempt and sentence them, without them having the chance to have their defence be heard, to make their own defence and thus being deprived of a fair proceeding against them. The Court in its arguments points to a potential right of the society as a whole to enjoy a functioning judicial system but argues such a right could not justify an infringement upon an individual's right to a fair hearing. The Court of Review further supports this citing previous precedent from amongst all judicial levels of SimDemocracy.

On the societies right to a functioning judicial system

[9] The question whether SimDemocracy is even a society to begin with is not easy to answer but if SimDemocracy would not be a Society or something equivalent to such, it could not hold a right to a functioning judicial system to begin with. The exact status of SimDemocracys status as a society has divided the Court resulting in two parallel concurrences, both converging again in their ultimate results.

On the further usage of society in the constitution

[10] Regardless of SimDemocracys status as a Society, it is questionable if it would be able to hold any rights. Art. 29 of the Constitution refers to “the people” as not being limited in their rights by the enumeration of rights in the Constitution. What “the people” encompasses once again is not defined. In re Impeachment of Wolf [2020] SDSC 8 [8] attaches “the people” to the group of people able to vote in impeachment proceedings, thus to all registered voters in SimDemocracy. Similarly Reference re Treaties [2021] SDSC 4 [13] attaches “the people” to every person able to vote in a referendum, once again this means registered votes. In re Replacement of KingRed31 [2020] SDSC 21 [15, 16.1f, 18] as well points to the people as being represented in parliament, thus the electorate of that body, analogous to modern SimDemocracy constitutional provisions that would be the voters, able to vote in senate elections. In re Replacement of KingRed31 [2020] SDSC 21 [13] uses, “the people” to refer to the people subject to criminal law under SimDemocracy jurisdiction. In re 9th Parliamentary Election [2021] SDSC 3 [6] takes an even wider stance, making “the people” out to be any person able to file an amicus brief, and thus essentially every human being if they chose to participate in the legal system of SimDemocracy.

[11] These opinions are largely mirrored in further precedent (see Reference re Voting by Proxy [2025] SDSC 5 [21] among others). All of this precedent attaches the “the people” to a definable group of individuals and mean to attach the groups importance to the individuals they are composed of, be it because the the individual is the one able to vote, or because it is the individual is able to file a brief, it does not mean a plentitude of people amalgamated into a larger group that has rights all of its own. Society as a sociological concept capturing multiple individuals into a shared plentitude thus is not able to hold rights of its own.

[11.1] Society as a concept is held as having protected rights in Art. 29 Constitution and thus the question needs to be asked, if not by that phrase alone, society is constituted as able to hold rights.
As established above, society is a diffuse concept and can not directly be applied in SimDemocracy. SimDemocracy could at best be qualified as a quasi-society. The problem, how such rights could persist, thus remains.
[11.2] Art. 29 Constitution aims to protect the population of the server from encroachment upon their rights, enumerated or not. It is a restriction upon the legislature for how and when to exercise its powers and. When the State uses its powers to intrude upon the Sphere of Rights of its Citizens, Art. 29 serves as a Test to determine if such an encroachment is justified. Therefore Art. 29 is both intentionally open and vague. It is meant to extend the greatest degree of protection possible and has to be interpreted when in doubt as being expansive. It covers the widest sphere of rights.
[11.3] Art. 29 aims to protect the expression of rights and regulates how the state may act to protect such exercises. It is not the general protection of being. Society as a mere sociological group can not exercise any rights, besides existence if such a capability would be assumed. Therefore, the phrasing needs to be understood as the most general protection of rights the constitution knows, the rights of all component individuals. The rights of the society are the same as the rights of all its members, enumerated and accumulated. Society does not hold rights on its own, rather it acts as a common test for actions to be measured on their impact upon a large number of members. Where an action might impose upon more than a singular person, by for example addressing a group or a class, and where such action might impose restrictions upon the exercise of rights, such may violate the rights of society by violating the rights of members in the group. In contrast where the state acts, it also needs to guarantee, protecting the rights of a singular person does not intrude upon the exercise of rights by the plurality of people, as such would violate the rights of each individual person in that group.
[11.4] Rights can not be held directly by society. Instead it derives validity as a test for the amalgamation or the rights derived from each component member. Where the Constitution refers to the rights of society, instead the rights of the component individuals need to be tested, through the sum of rights held by individuals the state is bound to protect them also as a greater whole and take such into consideration in its actions. Further see In re Fair Use of Alternate Accounts Act 2025 [2026] SDSC 1 [34.1], which has been answered here as far as it had been left open.

[12] Who else might be able to have a right to a functioning judicial system? Every Person already has the right to a fair hearing, this includes a functioning judicial system, a separate right to a judicial system overall thus will not be recognized here as it would be legislative to establish such a right if the same protection is already afforded by another right. The State could have the right to a functioning system though. The state is a defined legal person under the constitution but it too does not have a right to what is in essence one of its functions. The Constitution charges the state with certain duties, like upholding the three branches of power. A duty can not be construed to mean a right except insofar as a charge is accompanied by the right to exercise that duty. The state too does not have a right to a functioning judicial system.

On Contempt

[13] SimDemocracy knows 5 major spheres. Firstly the Supervisors sphere, the supervisors are tasked with unique duties in the constitution and carry unique legal obligations. Second the Public sphere, this encompasses all citizens and non-citizens interacting with SimDemocracy, it is governed by statutory law and can draw upon constitutional protections. Next the judicial sphere, sitting at the cross-section where the law and the population meet. It is governed by three sets of rules we will expand upon further in the following point. The executive is the fourth sphere, being tasked with discharging the laws while subject to its own executive legal obligations but still retaining the greatest degree of freedom under its executive privileges of all three branches of power. Lastly, the Legislative Sphere, the Senate and its guaranteed powers under the constitution, the Senate also retains the ability to oversee all other spheres and impose a framework upon them through statutory law or by initiating constitutional changes.

[14] The Courts operate in a unique set of circumstances. On the one hand they are bound by law, both statutory and constitutional. Every party is afforded certain rights and imposed duties. The Court too is charged with duties and the protection of rights in the course of proceedings. On the other hand, the judicial branch is its own branch of power and has the ability to regulate its own conduct and how affairs within its sphere are conducted, within statutory limits. This too is acknowledged in the Criminal Code, which lists inherent powers and coercive powers in Art. 21 Subdivision 1, Subsection 1 §2.

[15] Finding the Proceedings for Contempt of Court in the Criminal Code is highly unusual. Procedural law is usually found in the CPA or CPC. The Provisions in the Criminal Code combine Procedural law as well as material criminal law.

[16] Notwithstanding the structural positioning of Procedural law the court needs to apply in matters of contempt, the court is empowered through the Criminal Code to bring a criminal charge against a party.

[17] In re Penalties and Tickets Act [2025] SDSC 17 [11] clarifies that a full proceeding is not needed for every kind of punishment levied against a person. So if a court decides to use its coercive or punitive powers, no full criminal proceeding needs to be deployed. Contempt under the Criminal Code is a Criminal Charge though. According to In re Impeachment of Wolf [2020] SDSC 8 [10.1], which is upheld in In re Penalties and Tickets Act [2025] SDSC 17 [11], the fair hearing right fully applies where a criminal charge is brought. This means, if a person is held in direct contempt of court, they are entitled to a full hearing. This includes the right to take an attorney to represent them, have time to prepare their defence and have their arguments heard.

Regarding the specific question posed by the Court of Review

[18] The Question the Court of Review posed is in relation to an non-participating attorney in a proceeding. The Court errs in the very basis for its question. Art. 1 Subdivision 1 Subsection 2 Criminal Court specifies that it applies to a “party” in a proceeding. An Attorney is not their own party before the Court, they are part of the Party that retained them. If an Attorney falls inactive in judicial proceedings, they thus can not be charged with Direct Contempt of Court. The fitting charge is found in Subsection 3 of the Provision, Indirect Contempt of Court. A Court may find a person in Contempt of Court and then initiate the proceedings outlined in that Subsection, which include a full criminal trial, which satisfies the protection to a fair Hearing of the Attorney in question.

[19] Where the law provides for a punishment to be levied against a person for committing a crime, the statue has to be read with the greatest possible restraint. Criminal Punishment is the harshest exercise of state power into the protected sphere of rights of an individual as it can result in the very exercise of these rights being denied altogether. Should, therefore, ambiguity arise from a law, the courts are bound to interpret the statute in question in a way, most benefitting the accused party before the court, in line with the rule of in dubio pro reo. Applied to this case, as the law allows for a differentiation between the two users and one reading does prevent criminal prosecution, the courts are bound to follow that reading.

Decision

[20] Art. 21 Subdivision 1 Subsection 2 § 2 (b) Criminal Code is constitutional. Art. 21 Subdivision 1 Subsection 2 Criminal Code can not be used to bring a charge against a party's attorney. The correct charge against an inactive attorney would be Art. 21 Subdivision 1 Subsection 3 Criminal Code.

CONCURRING OPINION by Justice Terak

(with Justice TheLittleSparty agreeing)

[A1] It is highly questionable if the society as an entity exists or is able to even hold right if an existence of such a diffuse group would be assumed. The concept of “society” itself is not a settled issue within sociology, the court will use the following definition in taking judicial notice of the meaning of the Word and its meaning as a concept: “a definable group of entities sharing a common cultural and social background governed by a set of statutory and unspoken rules.” SimDemocracy does not allow for a defined society under this definition. As an online simulation, SimDemocracy does not have a defined outer boundary, it does not have a defined set of users and it does not form around a common cultural and social background. It more accurately is the intersection of personal spheres created for interactive and recreational purposes. The citizenry of SimDemocracy also is not able to hold the form of a society. Citizenship is not tied to actually partaking in the daily activities or reality of experience of SimDemocracy and could be reduced to simply registering to vote and voting every so often. The state also is not able to take the role of “society”, it is a defined legal entity and legal person, not just the group of its constituents. SimDemocracy could take the shape of a quasi-society though. A group that at least is able to be defined by its citizenship and governed by common statutory law.

CONCURRING OPINION by Chief Justice Ed

(with Justice Ivy Cactus agreeing)

[B1] Concerning the question about contempt, I believe the majority reached a correct conclusion. I write to address the mischaracterization of SimDemocracy in the concurrence by Justice Terak.

[B2] The majority's definition of a society ("a definable group of entities sharing a common cultural and social background governed by a set of statutory and unspoken rules") does not obviously exclude SimDemocracy. The majority points to the absence of a defined border and a defined set of users as disqualifying factors, I disagree with this characterisation. Many groupings we readily call “societies” lack strictly defined borders or a fixed set of members. SimDemocracy is no different. It operates under a common statutory framework, has developed its own norms, and its members participate in a shared political culture.

[B3] SimDemocracy is, in at least some sense, a society. The Constitution’s own reference to the “rights of society” would be incoherent if such a society was definitionally impossible or unintended. The majority's use of quasi-society is however not an unreasonable characterisation. Either way, the majority is right in its conclusion that society is not an entity able to hold rights independent of those coming from the members who make it up.
