Brandmal, ex parte State of SimDemocracy (Appellant) v Birdish (Respondent) [2025] SDCR 5

Date 4th October 2025
Judges
  • Chief Judge Benbookworm
  • Judge ppatpat
  • Judge hmquestionable
Held
  • The trial judge had no authority to hear the appeal of the contempt judgement.
  • The original sentence is reinstated.
Ruling 2-1
Applicable precedent
  • Contempt proceedings are independent of the larger trial they arise from, [3]
  • The Inferior Court does not have appellate jurisdiction over itself, [4]

MAJORITY OPINION by Judge ppatpat and Judge Hmquestionable

Introduction

[1] These proceedings arise out of a contempt proceeding initiated in the Inferior Court against the respondent, Birdish.

[2] At the end of the proceeding, the Inferior Court Judge sentenced Birdish to a 1 week mute. Upon appeal by counsel for Birdish, the Inferior Court Judge amended the sentence to a 2 day mute. The petitioner then appealed, for the reason that the Judge had no power to overturn his own verdict. Considerations

[3] Contempt proceedings are independent judicial proceedings. They are not mere motions within an underlying case but stand as distinct adjudications within themselves.

[4] Under Article 5 §3 of the CPA, the term "decisions" must be narrowly construed. The section explicitly defines “decisions” as those pertaining to motions. To expand this to include final verdicts — especially verdicts on contempt — would both distort the statutory language and risk eroding judicial finality. Contempt, by its nature, does not arise from a party motion but from the court's own authority; thus, the proper route of appeal, if any, lies with a higher court, not through reconsideration by the same judge.

Verdict

[5] We find for the State, and agree that the overturning of the original verdict by the Judge was invalid. The decision is therefore quashed, and the original sentence of a 1 week mute is therefore reinstated.

[6] This ruling makes no comment on the merits of the original appeal itself, merely that the trial judge had no authority to hear it.

DISSENTING OPINION by Chief Judge Benbookworm

[7] While the word appeal seems odd when combined with the court of first instance, this is merely a matter of the Senate not knowing legalese. Consider it as a motion to reconsider instead.

[8] As such, I dissent.
