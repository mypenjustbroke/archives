In re Article 3, Section 3 of the Citizenship Act [2021] SDSC 2

Date of judgment 28th February 2021
Justices
  • Chief Justice Ivy Cactus
  • Justice Syndicality
Held The section was constitutional.
Ruling 2-0
Applicable precedent
  • One does not automatically have SimDemocracy citizenship, but they do have the right to gain it fairly and equally, [10]
  • Possessive “their”s in laws generally refer to hypothetical circumstances where one has the thing in question, not that one automatically has the thing in question under all circumstances, [11]
  • Naturalization requirements have the same meaning as restrictions from citizenship, [13]
  • The Right to citizenship restrictions being proportionate refers to the all naturalization options as a whole, rather than a specific option in isolation, [14]
  • Prevention of Election Fraud is a legitimate reason to restrict citizenship, [17]

MAJORITY OPINION by Chief Justice Ivy Cactus

(with Justice Syndicality agreeing)

Intro

[1] The petitioner is seeking review into Article 3, Section 3 of the Citizenship Act on grounds of it infringing the Right to Citizenship and the need for restrictions to citizenship to be proportional, as per Article 21 of the Constitution.

Summary of the petition

[2] The petitioner opens by stating that the Constitution states that everyone has the right not to be “restricted from their citizenship”, and because the “their” is possessive every person must automatically have SimDemocracy Citizenship, and therefore restrictions are unconstitutional.

[3] They then argue that the restrictions to naturalization under the section in question are not proportionate, due to the following reasons:

[3.1] The ability for one to gain citizenship if they have 150 Reddit karma means that public opinion may sway one's ability to gain citizenship due to Reddit’s voting system, and that “lurkers” may not be able to become citizens.
[3.2] The ability for one to gain citizenship if they have 1000 messages on the SimDemocracy discord server is not a valid reason to restrict one from citizenship, as without citizenship they have no reason to send messages.
[3.3] The ability for one to gain citizenship if endorsed by a government official would be difficult for one to gain, especially if they didn’t priorly know a government official.
[3.4] The ability for one to gain citizenship following a Voice Chat (VC) interview with the Immigration Officer is unrelated, as “no other function of r/SimDemocracy requires a citizen to participate in a verbal interview or related activity.”

Summary of the Response

[4] The respondent opens by stating that the “right to gain citizenship” is not equal to the “right to citizenship”.

[4.1] They argue that this can be seen through how the section was changed from “Every person shall have the right to citizenship.” to “Every person shall have the right to gain citizenship.”, they explained that this change occurred due to a discussion between the author “Ivy Cactus” and legal expert, Former Chief Justice, and curator of the Case Law Index “Trolligrach (Danyo)”, in which they discuss that the change was necessary and expressly made to accommodate the act restricting citizenship.
[4.2] The respondent continues to clarify that the “right to gain citizenship” does not provide an absolute right to citizenship, but a right to “gain” citizenship. They explain that this entails equal and fair access to citizenship, meaning that every person is entitled to have a chance to gain citizenship by the same means as every other person.

[5] The respondent then replies to the petitioners claims that the karma requirement is not proportionate, stating that due to it being an activity requirement only trolls and inactive accounts would be below the karma requirement and therefore unable to gain citizenship through this method.

[5.1] They attempt to prove this by showing that the median karma of all Reddit accounts is 1968.
[5.2] The respondent would later explain that they don’t believe any of the petitioners points on proportionate are valid, as “restriction” refers to punishment for a crime, but they believed the petitioner’s point on Karma requirements was particularly bad.

Questions Asked by the Court

[6] The court asked multiple questions to the counsels. These questions were based off of the current internal arguments of the court, and were asked to help shape the court’s internal discussions.

[7] The first question the court asked was "The court is debating whether proportionate in this context doesn't refer to each individual way to naturalize, but rather the options as a whole. What are the counsels thoughts on this discussion?”.

[7.1] The petitioner explained that they believe the restrictions aren’t proportionate in any way, due to them being too arbitrary and strict for their purpose of preventing election fraud.
[7.2] The respondent explained that they believe that the section doesn’t concern naturalization, rather restriction from one’s citizenship is being used in the same way as “deprivation”, and is merely there to clarify it’s partner in the section.

[8] The second question the court asked was “The court is debating whether proportionate restriction is equal to punishment, or if it refers to another thing such as protection of other rights. What are the counsels thoughts on this discussion?.

[8.1] The petitioner explained that they believe the “Deprivation” in the section is in reference to punishment, and the “Restriction” refers to reasons one may not be naturalized.
[8.2] The respondent explained that they believe “Restriction” must necessarily be punishment for a crime, given that deprivation and restriction is in the domain of criminal law.

The Meaning of the “Right to Gain Citizenship”

[9] The court will first be addressing whether the “Right to Gain Citizenship” means that there may not be naturalization requirements, as argued by the petitioner.

[10] The court believes that it’s clear by the wording that this section refers to one being able to get citizenship equally and fairly, and not that every person is automatically given SimDemocracy citizenship.

[10.1] This is confirmed by how the section was changed with the amendment that was included as part of the Citizenship Act, and further expressed by the author’s comments that this was the purpose of the section, such as those presented by the respondent.

[11] The possessive “their” doesn’t confirm that one may automatically have citizenship either, since it is clearly referring to the hypothetical persons citizenship in the hypothetical, not saying everyone has it automatically.

[11.1] Generally sections of the constitution with “their” refer to a hypothetical person and their hypothetical rights, which are entirely based on circumstances, and are easier read such as “Their hypothetical citizenship” or “Their hypothetical defense”; this is shown clearly in sections such as Section 4 of Article 18 of the Constitution, “Every person shall have the right to adequate time for the preparation of their defence, and the right to private correspondence and communications with legal counsel.”, it would not make sense to assume that everyone automatically has to have time for a defense they don’t have, it just refers to a defense they may hypothetically have if they in the future have a defense, similarly to how one may in the future have citizenship.

[12] Therefore, Article 21 doesn’t automatically guarantee everyone citizenship, and rather grants the ability for everyone to fairly and equally have access to gaining it.

Are the Naturalization Requirements Proportionate?

[13] Before moving forward the court would like to make it clear that it believes requirements for naturalization in this case is entirely equal to restriction from citizenship, it’s two sides of the same coin. If one is restricted from their citizenship by a requirement they are required to surpass that requirement to gain citizenship, you may not have one without the other.

[14] The court believes that due to the constitution specifying restriction from citizenship as a whole in this section, rather than stating that each possible restriction from citizenship must be proportionate in of itself.

[14.1] That being said to determine whether or not these restrictions as a whole are proportionate the court will examine them individually, and then see if they together give a variety of accessible and reasonable options to choose from.

[15] For that purpose the court will be judging each individual requirement on its purpose and how reasonable it is for a person to achieve it.

[15.1] The karma requirement is used as a judgement of how active one is on Reddit, an integral part of SimDemocracy. The karma cutoff is far below the average karma and frankly extremely easy to achieve.
[15.2] The message requirement is used as a judgement of how active one is on the SimDemocracy discord, with it being a reasonable and numerical way to judge one's contribution to Simdem’s community. It is hard to judge how difficult it is for one to achieve this number, but given there are members of the community who have 124 times it, it is clearly not an unreasonable number.
[15.3] The government endorsement is a judgement of how active one is in the government, and can be used to assess the quality of one’s contribution to Simdemocracy. It should be noted that one does not need to be a citizen to join a government office, and therefore can fulfill this section without priorly knowing a government official.
[15.4] The Voice Chat requirement checks if one is an individual, meaning that it makes sure one person doesn’t get the same citizenship on multiple accounts by verifying the same voice isn’t used to gain multiplied citizenships.

[16] These options in whole give people a variety of ways to prove they are a real person, with one only needing to be active on Reddit, active on Discord, active in government, or willing to talk to become a citizen.

[16.1] Given the clear variety and ease of achievement for these options, it is easy to say that the options proportionally gauge if one is a real person or able to contribute to SimDemocracy.

[17] To quickly touch on legitimacy of restrictions, some restrictions are instrumental in preventing voter fraud, contradictory to the petitioner’s claims. This can be seen in how the court was able to raise the standard of evidence used in the removal of fraudulent votes after the amendment passed (See In re 4th Parliamentary Election).

Conclusion

[18] While persons have the right to equally and fairly be able to gain citizenship, they do not have the right for citizenship to be automatically given.

[19] The current naturalization requirements are both proportional and legitimate.

Verdict

[20] Article 3, Section 3 of the Citizenship act is constitutional, and the petition is dismissed.
