Writ of Execution of "Lucas v Iaccp Bank & Trust [2025] Civ 37" [2026] SDCR 9

Date of judgment 16th January 2026
Judge Court of Review Judge Brandmal
Ruling Writ of Execution is granted
Applicable precedent
  • Writs of Execution are a subset of Writs of Enforcement.
  • A Writ of Enforcement is in order where enforcement is (still) necessary and there are no orders for Enforcement from the original Court or where the Orders of the Court are genuinely subjectively unclear.

DECISION by Judge Brandmal

Introduction and Argumentation

[1] The Petitioner requests a Writ of Execution for the Verdict in _lucasdaboi_ v Iaccp Bank & Trust [2025] Civ 37. They petition this Court, as the Department of Treasury has previously denied executing the Verdict to their satisfaction. The relevant portion thereof reads: 

“Remedy
This Court orders the defendant to pay the plaintiff a total of 2,000 tau, calculated as follows:
1,058 tau as equal to the principal plus interest accrued
942 tau as enhanced damages and costs
The Department of Treasury is authorized to execute this judgment against any accounts or assets held in the name of Iaccp Bank and Trust. Should such assets prove insufficient, further enforcement proceedings may be sought consistent with the Civil Code.”

[2] The Court has called the Secretary to testify on why they refused to comply with the Petitioners wishes. They argued that they were genuinely uncertain what to do and advised by the Trial Judge to wait for a Writ of Enforcement.

Considerations on Writs of Enforcement generally

[3] The Court generally does not believe a Writ of Enforcement is strictly necessary to execute a Court Order or Verdict. Such decisions are generally enforceable without further Judicial orders (see Art. 14 §1. CPA and Art. 9 §1. of the Constitution, as otherwise not much authority would emanate from the Judiciary). However, the lawmaker, in The Schedule and Art. 3 §1.1. Of the Judiciary Act, saw fit to allow this Court to order the authorities to execute Verdicts of the Inferior Court where they fall under continuing Jurisdiction. It is therefore necessary to differentiate where the authorities refusing to act in accordance with a Verdict and demanding a Writ of Enforcement is Contempt of Court and where it is lawful.

[4] In SDCR 28: Opinion and Writ of Execution the Court laid out some aspects to this important difference, but did not fully articulate them. There, the debtor was unable to pay, as they were banned. The Court stated that “a lack of payout is a lack of enforcement”. It can therefore be inferred, that a debtor not paying requires enforcement and that the Court may provide the basis for such enforcement. This is generally sufficient for a Writ of Enforcement. The extrapolation from this is therefore that, where a Verdict finds a debt to exist (due to liability for example) and gives no basis or means of enforcement, a Writ of Enforcement in some way is in order.

[5] There now remains the question on what to do if the Verdict is not entirely clear on how to execute it. To calm the Executive, in order for the act of refusing to execute a Verdict to be Contempt, intent and a sufficiently clear order (which more likely than not is a high bar in a criminal process) is necessary, genuine uncertainty cannot suffice. Likewise, the Executive cannot be required to follow unclear orders and in such cases, a Writ of Enforcement is also required if only to clarify the orders. In order to protect the Right of the creditor of the original verdict they received from said verdict, this must also apply if the relevant executive officer only subjectively believes the Orders to be clear and therefore refuse. Abuse of this principle can be avoided since then intentionality becomes virulent again and hence Contempt is a possibility.

[6] To summarise, a Writ of Enforcement is in order where enforcement is (still) necessary and there are no orders for Enforcement from the original Court or where the Orders of the Court are genuinely subjectively unclear.

Considerations on the applicability on this case

[7] Consequently the Court must now apply these principles. Chief Judge Thyme in their original Verdict ordered the plaintiff to pay a sum of 2,000 tau. They also authorised execution against the Banks and Trusts accounts. The Secretary did so, by paying all remaining tau, but they proved insufficient. Now, the Verdict instructs “Should such assets prove insufficient, further enforcement proceedings may be sought consistent with the Civil Code.”. The Treasurer now claimed to be uncertain what to make of this Provision and therefore requested a Writ of Execution. As explained above, such uncertainty is sufficient absent signs of deception.

[8] The Procedure outlined in SDCR 28: Opinion and Writ of Execution can be applied analogously and was indeed requested by the petitioner. No material defense was offered, except impossibility, which seems far fetched, considering the potential value of the name of the business. A Writ of Execution is a valid way of Enforcement (and a subcategory of a Writ of Enforcement) and as such ordered in the following manner:

Verdict

WRIT OF EXECUTION by Judge Brandmal

IT IS HEREBY AUTHORISED, ORDERED AND COMMANDED, pursuant to the Court’s authority to enforce civil judgments, that the Department of the Treasury shall:

  1. Identify any and all assets belonging to Iaccp Bank and Trust, including likeness, ownership and name in one bundle.
  2. Seize and liquidate said assets via public auction.
  3. Distribute the proceeds (up to 2,000 tau) to _lucasdaboi_ in full.
  4. Should there be any more than 2,000 tau available from liquidation, the remainder shall be returned to the legal entity.
  5. Provide certification of compliance to the Court of Review.

SO ORDERED

Dated: January 17th, 2026

Brandmal

Judge Brandmal, Court of Review
