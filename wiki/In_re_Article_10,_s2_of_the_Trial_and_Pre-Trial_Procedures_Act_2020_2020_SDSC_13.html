In re Article 10, s2 of the Trial and Pre-Trial Procedures Act 2020 [2020] SDSC 13

Date of judgment 9th September 2020
Justices
  • Chief Justice Danyo
  • Justice CreatingKing
  • Acting Justice SeaOtter
Held Article 10, s2 of the Trial and Pre-Trial Procedures Act 2020 was unconstitutional because it unconstitutionally violated the right to a fair hearing
Ruling 3-0
Applicable precedent
  • Reaffirms principle of leniency for court procedure under NightEye (Appellant) v LordDeadlyOwl (Respondent) [2020] SDSC 5, [8], [11]-[12.1]
  • If the Supreme Court issues an amendment proposal for unconstitutional statute, the Senate must accept it under the Supreme Court Procedures Act within a certain timeframe or the unconstitutional provisions are voided by automatic operation of law, [14.2]
  • The aforementioned time frame is seven (7) days as per In re SDBI and SDIA Acts [2020] SDSC 2 [23], [14.2]

MAJORITY OPINION by Acting Justice SeaOtter

(with Chief Justice Danyo and Justice CreatingKing agreeing)

Introduction

[1] The petitioner is seeking judicial review of Article 10, s2 of the Trial and Pre-trial Procedures Act of 2020 (“TAPPA”) on grounds that it conflicts with citizens’ Article 19 right to a fair hearing and on the grounds that it is inconsistent with prior Supreme Court precedent.

[2] The section in question establishes that should a party to a civil hearing fail to appear at the scheduled time, the case shall be found in favor of the present party, stating “§2. Should either party fail to be present at the scheduled time, the hearing will be automatically be found in favour of the party present.”

Summary of Arguments

[3] The petitioner states two (2) arguments in this review.

[4] The first argument is that under Article 19, s3 of the Constitution, “§3. Every person shall have the right to defend themselves in person, and every person shall have the right to legal counsel for their defence.” The petitioner argues that a judge ruling primarily on the attendance of a party places “an undue burden on the defendant to show up at the arranged time” on the defendant.

[4.1] The petitioner also notes that the TAPPA section does not specify what failing to appear means, and suggests that it could mean anything, such as being two minutes late.

[5] The second argument of the petitioner is that section 2 falls under previous Supreme Court precedent, specifically NightEye (Appellant) v LordDeadlyOwl (Respondent) [2020] SDSC 5.

[5.1] In Nighteye [9], the court ruled that “Section 3 of Article 26 [of the Constitution, now Article 19] allows every person to defend themselves in person or with legal counsel. Justice Euphyrric summarily finding for the plaintiff because the defendant did not show up infringed on this right.” The court also ruled that when procedure rules are so strict that they cannot accommodate real life emergencies, it infringes on the right to a fair hearing, Nighteye [13].

[6] The petitioner argues that, in response, the court should strike down Article 10, section 2.

Summary of the response

[7] The respondent presented a counter-argument, stating that while all citizens have the right to a fair hearing, by not showing up at the agreed upon time, the absent party forfeits that right.

[7.1] However, the respondent agrees with the petitioner, in that virtual court cases in r/SimDemocracy should not trump more important obligations in real life.

[8] The respondent proposed a different remedy from the petitioner. Instead, the court should suggest an edit to the section, allowing for exceptions to the rule to account for real life commitments and responsibilities.

Burdens to the parties

[9] The petitioner argued that Article 10, s2 placed an undue burden on the defendant to appear in court, and should he not, the case would be found against him. The court finds that this very clearly infringes on the defendant’s right to defend themselves under Article 19 of the Constitution.

Exercising one's Article 19 Rights

[10] As was established in [7], the respondent argued that one can forfeit their right to a fair hearing through inaction, such as one forfeits their right to suffrage in not voting.

[10.1] The court disagrees, believing that one’s right to a fair hearing is sacrosanct to the judicial system, and that someone should not be punished for being otherwise engaged in real life.

The ambit of Article 19, s3

[11] While every person has the right to defend themselves in court, a defendant maliciously avoiding a hearing to prevent a remedy from being reached needlessly harms both the public interest, as well as the plaintiff’s right to a fair hearing, as was ruled in Nighteye [12].

[11.1] In cases where a party is intentionally avoiding a civil hearing, the court believes that their right to defend themselves can be infringed to continue with a proper hearing.

Conclusion

[12] The court reaffirms the precedent under Nighteye [9], in that summarily ruling for the plaintiff when the defendant does not show up infringes on the defendant's right to a fair hearing. However, if a party is repeatedly or deliberately avoiding a hearing, their right to defend themselves may be infringed as their actions infringe on the right of the plaintiff to a fair hearing.

[12.1] The court also upholds that real life emergencies and other similar issues take precedence over standard r/SimDemocracy court procedure.

Verdict

[13] Article 10, section 2 of the Trial and Pre-trial Procedures Act, as currently written, is unconstitutional.

[14] Under the Supreme Court Procedures Act (“SCPA”), Article 1, section 7.1, the court suggests the following amendment be added to Article 10, section 2 of TAPPA as section 2.3.

[14.1] “§2.3. If a party had a reasonable excuse for their absence and could not reasonably give notice, this section shall not apply and the hearing shall be rescheduled.”
[14.2] Under the SCPA, the Senate must have the opportunity to approve this amendment, which shall be done through the normal process. If the Senate fails to do so, Article 10, s2 will be null, void, and of no effect by automatic operation of law. As the SCPA does not specify the time in which the amendment must be approved, this court shall defer to the precedent under In re SDBI and SDIA Acts [2020] SDSC 2 [23], and give a seven (7) day implementation period before Article 10, s2 is voided automatically.
