Question of law for a definitive order or proceedings [2025] SDCR 32

Date of judgment 28th November 2025
Judges
  • Judge Brandmal
Held Court orders of different Judges in the same Court are still orders of the same Court.
Ruling 1-0
Applicable precedent

MAJORITY OPINION by Judge Brandmal

(with Judge Brandmal agreeing and Judge Brandmal being unsure in parts)

Introduction

[1] The questions of law were posed by Judge Terak regarding an ongoing civil trial:

[1.1.] “At which part in the trial may arguments be given? May arguments be presented alongside the presentation of evidence and the cross thereof or should such arguments be preserved for the closing statement. (Question regarding the CPA in relation to the order of steps in a civil trial)”
[1.2.] “Questions as to the binding effect of judicial orders on a proceedings [sic], especially if there has [sic] been multiple changes in who presides and which order need adhered to, may be replaced or amended and if such is within a newer judicial authorities scope. (Question as to the scope of judicial power in applying and making orders in trial).”

[2] Due to the need for immediate action, as the trial had already stalled for some time, the Court issued a temporary ruling, where it allowed for presenting main arguments but still following the steps as present in the CPA, by using broader definitions for them. Judge Terak was also ordered to consider the orders of previous trial Judges as orders of the same Court, that could be amended and, where contradicting, where only the most recent order ought to be followed.

Considerations and Verdict

[3] The first question has resolved itself thanks to an amendment to the CPA, what a happy coincidence.

[4] The second question however remains. The Court firstly turns to the law. When a Judge is replaced, Art. 6 CPA speaks of a change of the Judge, but not a change of Court. Hence, even if several Judges are replaced during the same trial, the same Court is still in session. That is necessary, otherwise the “new” Court might have to rehear relevant arguments. Art. 14 CPA however pertains to Judges having the ability to issue “Court Orders”. For once, the nomenclature is consistent and meaningful. Reading all of this together it is apparent, that a “Court” is not just the Judge and therefore, even if the Judge is exchanged, the Court orders still stand on valid grounds. In the eyes of the law, nothing of relevance has changed, as the personality of the Judge is a nonfactor.

[5] It also follows, that contradicting orders ought to be resolved as if they were issued by the same Judge (as they were issued by the same Court). The CPA does not prohibit a Court from changing it’s opinion or orders. Where they contradict, they are considered supplanted, amended or quashed and ought to be handled accordingly. The principle of lex posterior (the newer law takes precedent over the older law) can be applied analogously.
