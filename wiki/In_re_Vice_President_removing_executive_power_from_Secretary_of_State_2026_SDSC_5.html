Summary Decision - In re Vice President removing executive power from Secretary of State [2026] SDSC 5

Date of judgment 7th February 2026
Justices
  • Chief Justice Ed
  • Justice Terak
  • Justice Ivy Cactus
  • Justice Britz
  • Justice TheLittleSparty
Held
  • The Vice President possesses independent, constitutionally-sanctioned authority over Executive Officers
  • The restriction of operational authority is not tantamount to the dismissal of an Executive Officer from their position.
Ruling 5-0
Applicable precedent The Vice President, as the constitutionally designated surrogate of operational oversight, may issue binding orders to Executive Officers, including the restriction or modulation of the operational powers of an Executive Officer, [5.2]

MAJORITY OPINION by Justice Britz

(with Chief Justice Ed, Justice IvyCactus, Justice TheLittleSparty and Justice Terak, agreeing)

Introduction

[1] The core question presented in the petition is whether the Vice President retains the constitutionally mandated authority to restrict the operational powers of a Cabinet-level Executive Officer. Summary of the Petition

[2] The petitioner contends:

[2.1] The executive authority exercised by the Secretary of State is derived solely from presidential delegation.
[2.2] That the removal of authority from the Secretary of State is functionally equivalent to dismissal.
[2.3] Article 3 §1 of the Executive Departments Act 2025 limits supervision and direction of the Secretary of State exclusively to the President.

Summary of the Response

[3] This case was heard summarily by the Court, and as such invited no response.

Constitutional Structure of the Executive

[4] Article 6 §1 of the Constitution establishes the Vice President as “the secondary executive body of SimDemocracy…wielding executive authority second only to the President”. Article 7 §2 further provides that Executive Officers possess authority “behind only the authority of the President and the Vice-President”. These provisions establish a rigorous constitutional hierarchy within the Executive of SimDemocracy, wherein authority emanates from the President downward, with the Vice President constitutionally positioned superior to all other Executive Officers. The Vice President is empowered with all operational prerogatives available to the President, save those expressly reserved to the President by constitutional or statutory mandate. Thus, the Vice President is rendered the functional second-in-command of the Executive branch.

On the Powers of the Vice President

[5] Article 7 §3 of the Constitution states that “members of the executive may issue orders which those with lower authority are compelled to obey”. Although Article 3 §1 of the EDA establishes the Secretary of State acts “under the direction of the President”, this statutory language does not circumscribe or derogate the Vice President’s authority, for three reasons;

[5.1] As established in [4], the Constitution places the Vice President above all other Executive Officers, and Constitutional authority cannot be overridden by statutory language. Thus, Executive Officers constitute “those of lower authority” within the meaning of Article 7 §3 in relation to the Vice President. Therefore, while Article 3 §1 recognises the President’s ultimate supervisory accountability over the Secretary of State, it does not diminish the hierarchical authority vested in the Vice President by the Constitution.
[5.2] As explained in In re Article 4 of the Executive Act 2023 [2025] SDSC 9 [31][32], SimDemocracy is primarily governed by a “theory” commonly referred to as “Unitary Executive”, which allows the President to delegate operational oversight. The Vice President, as the constitutionally designated surrogate of such oversight, may issue executive orders to Executive Officers, including the restriction or modulation of the operational powers of an Executive Officer.
[5.3] The Court rejects the proposition that the presidential appointment of an individual to the position of an Executive Officer constitutes a presidential order permitting the appointee to fully exercise the theoretical scope of their powers, immune from Vice Presidential oversight or restriction. It is the belief of the Court that appointment is simply the conferral of a title and office onto an individual, not an automatic empowerment to exercise every possible power of the office at all times. Considering the counterfactual - that appointment did confer such irrevocable authority - Executive Officers, solely by appointment, would be removed from the hierarchical structure of the Executive and be placed as equals with the Vice President, in direct violation of Article 7 §2 of the Constitution. Nonetheless, it should be said that Presidential authority over the Vice President is retained, as the President may, by an express executive order, direct the Vice President to refrain from interfering in the operations of an Executive Officer, thereby retaining the ability to countermand the Vice President’s actions. This is in line with Article 6 §1 of the Constitution, which places the Vice President “second only to the President.”

On the difference between “suspension” and “dismissal”

[6] Whilst framed in the context of the Acting Presidency, Article 5 §5.1.1.1 - 5.1.1.3 of the Constitution delineates the distinction between “suspension” and “dismissal”. Suspension is the “temporary cessation of the powers and duties of an office without creating a vacancy and without termination the incumbent’s title to the office” and the “Incumbent retains the title to the office and remains the lawful office-holder.”, but cannot “exercise any authority, powers, prerogatives, or vote of the suspended office while suspended.” These provisions demonstrate that the loss of operational powers may occur without an explicit termination of office - the Secretary of State remained appointed and continued to occupy the office.

Decision

[7] The Court holds that the Vice President possesses independent, constitutionally-sanctioned authority over Executive Officers, and subsequently, may issue binding orders to subordinate Executive Officers, including, but not limited to, the restriction of operational authority. The Court also holds that the restriction of operational authority is not tantamount to the dismissal of an Executive Officer from their position.
