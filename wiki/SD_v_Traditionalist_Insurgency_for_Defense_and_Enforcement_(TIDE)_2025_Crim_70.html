SD v Traditionalist Insurgency for Defense and Enforcement (TIDE) [2025] Crim 70

Date of judgment 7 June 2025
Judge Justice Ivy Cactus
Charges 1 charge of Conspiracy to Commit TOS Violations each
Verdict All guilty on all charges
Sentence Permanent bans for each defendant
Applicable persuasive precedent Enough circumstantial evidence of being included in a conspiracy can be considered a tacit “offer of material assistance”, [24]

JUDGMENT by Justice Ivy Cactus<ref>Verdict redacted for national security purposes, if still redacted on or after 7 November 2025 contact Ivy Cactus for the unredacted version</ref>

Introduction

[1] The Department of Justice is charging over 20 accounts associated with the defunct political party “Traditionalist Insurgency for Defense and Enforcement”, commonly referred to simply as “TIDE” (formerly the Traditionalist Defense and Vanguard Party or TDVP), with Conspiracy to Commit ToS Violations. The State sought a permanent ban for each of the accused.

[2] This trial is being held as required under GEETSA following the accounts’ preliminary bans stemming from an ECTPO for terrorism issued on April 13th of this year. As required under said act, the trial was held jointly for all defendants.

Procedural Notes

[3] This case was handled in a private court due to the highly classified nature of the evidence. This presented an issue because under GEETSA, the defendants must remain banned for the duration of proceedings, and therefore must receive a public defender, none of whom possessed appropriate clearance. In order to ensure a fair trial regardless, the President granted clearance to current Bar Director, and “prolific public defender” (In re Criminal Code Article 3, Section 3 [2025] SDSC 8 [5.1]), Palmer. The court is confident that this appointment and subsequent defense were enough to uphold the defendant’s Constitutional rights.

[4] At the beginning of pre-trial, the prosecution motioned for Summary Trial, which the defense did not contest. Summary Trials, as described in the Courtroom Procedures Act 2025, are trials handled without a pre-trial where evidence may be objected to as it is entered. In a case such as this, where there are no objections to the evidence as entered, skipping pre-trial as such is expeditious to ensure a speedy resolution to trial, and the court thanks both Palmer and the Attorney General, Delulu, for professionalism in handling it as such.

[5] Summary Trials, generally, are limited to a maximum of a 3-month ban as punishment — regardless of the charges. This requirement, however, may be waived if the State can prove, on the preponderance of the evidence, that the accused joined SimDemocracy with malicious intent and the accused had been on the Discord Server for less than 3 months. The prosecution forwarded that the nature of the “Subversive Doctrine” included in evidence, which shall be touched on later, the defendants’ intentions were clearly malicious. The defense raised no objections to this, and the 3-month maximum was waived.

Summary of the Evidence

[6] The evidence in this case is classified due to the high probability that releasing it prejudices ongoing operations or puts law enforcement officers in danger. Due to this, the evidence upon which this case relies will not be made public in the near future. In order to establish the grounds for the case to the public, despite this classification, I will endeavor to summarize the evidence presented to the court without revealing too much.

[7] The first piece of evidence is a document containing a list of users in a TIDE operation server, [REDACTED] This list contains screenshots of all 20 defendants’ user profiles with their Discord IDs attached.

[8] The rest of the evidence is included in an affidavit filed by law enforcement official [REDACTED] — with screenshots interspersed with personal commentary on how it is relevant or what it demonstrates. The first piece of evidence with this affidavit is TIDE leader, “ready2endrose”, [REDACTED]. One can see, in said screenshots, that the server is titled “subversive operations” and there is [REDACTED]. There is also [REDACTED], roles such as “operator”, [REDACTED].

[9] The “Subversive Operations” document outlines a TIDE operation to “penetrate, destabilize, and dismantle” SimDemocracy “from within.” The operations include detailed plans to use alt-accounts to infiltrate SimDemocracy’s government and “act simultaneously to disrupt moderation, overload the system, and create administrative chaos.”

[9.1] As an aside, this document is very clearly AI-generated. The court believes that this does not represent a lack of authenticity of the evidence and can more easily be attributed to a lack of skill in planning and writing on the part of TIDE’s leadership. There is no reason to believe that this means they did not plan on using the document as a genuine outline for their plan.

Defendant’s Plea

[10] Summary trials are abnormal in that the defense is able to plead guilty at any point during the proceedings. In their closing statements, the defense chose to do so for the 11 accounts listed as participating in the “phases” of the subversive operation, as described in [7] and [9].

[11] The defense and prosecution both recommended a permanent ban for these 11 users. The court sees no special reasoning or mitigating circumstances that could be applied (other than a guilty plea, which the court does not find persuasive because of its technical nature), and, given the undisputedly malicious nature of these actions, concurs. Said users will receive a permanent ban for Conspiracy to Commit ToS violations.

On Criminal Jurisdiction

[13] The defense raised the argument that these actions happened entirely on a server outside of SimDemocracy and, thus, the courts do not possess jurisdiction over them. These charges, they argued, are functionally the same as charges being brought against somebody for an action that occurred on the Voices of Democracy (VoD) Discord server — if not more absurd because we have official relations with VoD.

[14] The State countered that Criminal Code Article 1 §4 defines a “person as any user who has, or had, the capacity and intention of participating in SimDemocracy,” and that “§5 further provides that if a crime, which directly affects SimDemocracy or persons in SimDemocracy, is committed outside of the jurisdiction given in this article, it may be prosecuted regardless, provided the person under prosecution satisfies the principles laid out in §4.”

[15] The court concurs with the prosecution. The “Subversive Operations” document provided a thorough plan for the members involved with the conspiracy to join and participate in SimDemocracy (even if said participation involved overthrowing the state); it would be absurd to say that SimDemocracy doesn’t have the authority to prosecute such plans. A simpler explanation would be that the crime that they were conspiring to commit would occur on the SimDemocracy discord server, and therefore, SimDemocracy retains jurisdiction.

[15.1] Near identical cases have been adjudicated before, such as SD v Joseph_Memestar [2020] Crim 9 and SD v GxOZxerd [2020] Crim 10, where users who had conspired to commit treason on unrelated discord servers (those for r/SimFascism and r/WeeklyDictator respectively) were charged and either convicted or let off on issues unrelated to jurisdiction. Conspiracy to commit crimes in SimDemocracy should be treated as if said crimes had already occurred in SimDemocracy.

Would the Actions Violate ToS

[16] The prosecution makes the simple argument that all of the accounts were complicit in the operational goals of the document outlined in [9]. These goals, the State posits, are ToS violations. The members of the server, then, all engaged in active conspiracy to commit ToS violations. It should be noted that

[17] The court must, of course, determine whether these actions would genuinely constitute ToS violations. The court thinks [REDACTED] put it best in [REDACTED] approval of the ECTPO that led to the preliminary bans in this case: “Preparing and creating a large set of accounts and gathering them in a coordinated manner to potentially deploy them to destabilise SimDem as a Discord Server and harass its members satisfy this standard as they go beyond "regular" harassment as understood by the ToS.”

[18] The court, therefore, affirms that the plans represented a violation of ToS, a gross and insidious one at that.

Did the [REDACTED] Nine Commit Conspiracy?

[19] This is the core of the defendant’s argument. They believe that the State has not proven that [REDACTED] committed conspiracy beyond a reasonable doubt.

[19.1] For reference purposes, the charge of conspiracy is defined as when someone “order[s]or request[s], or otherwise direct[s] a person to commit a criminal offense; or [assists] or [offers] material assistance in committing a crime.”

[20] The prosecution counters that, even if not directly mentioned, these people were members of TIDE and gave a continued tacit offer to assist in committing the subversive doctrine outlined in the document outlined in [9]. Continued participation in the server and organization, they reason, can not be taken as a neutral act.

[21] It is long-established law that merely being present in a Discord server while conspiracy occurs can not be considered conspiracy in and of itself. This precedent stems all the way back to SD v Joseph_Memestar [2020] Crim 9, where the defendant was found not guilty because they only agreed that the co-conspirators should commit the crime, and did not offer to help.

[22] The court sees a core difference between that case and this one: the conspiracy in Joseph_Memestar occurred on the r/SimFascism discord server. Said server existed to do things other than conspire to commit treason within SimDemocracy. The same can not be said about the server where these actions occurred. The server was titled “Subversive Operations”; the channels that the screenshots include are called [REDACTED]. The server existed for the sole purpose of committing this conspiracy. The roles that the accused had were “asset” and “operator”.

[23] The court is under the impression that it can’t be proven beyond a reasonable doubt that these “ [REDACTED] nine” were actually going to directly assist in the subversive operations. The crime of conspiracy, though, allows conviction if one merely “offer[s] material assistance” in committing a crime. It should be noted that this offer is not stated to have to be explicit, nor is it stated that said offer needs to be large.

[24] Allowing one to be labeled an asset or operator, being in a discord dedicated solely to committing massive and egregious ToS violations, continuing to identify as part of a group actively planning to commit terrorism: all of these are at least tacit offers of material assistance. One does not need to say “How can I help you commit crimes?” to offer assistance. There is no reasonable doubt that those agents and operators were offering to assist in the operations that the Discord server was dedicated to — even if they were not selected to participate in any of the first four phases of the operation.

[24.1] This is, certainly, on the fine line of reasonable doubt. Beyond a reasonable doubt does not mean “no possible other explanation”. Sometimes the circumstantial evidence builds upon itself until there is only one way to look at and understand the evidence without jumping through insane logical hoops. The court can not conceive of a scenario where the defendants were being labelled assets and operators and continuing to exist in a server dedicated to terrorism, but simultaneously not offering to assist in said terrorism in any way. Indeed, bolstering TIDE’s ranks is, in and of itself, a form of material assistance in the conspiracy they were planning on undertaking.

[25] The court, therefore, finds these “[REDACTED] nine” guilty of conspiracy to commit ToS Violations. Given the same circumstances apply as in the guilty pleas, and that the mitigating factor of the guilty plea does not apply, the court sentences these defendants to a permanent ban as well.

Verdict

[26] All 20 of the defendants are found guilty of conspiracy to commit ToS Violations and sentenced to permanent bans.

Postscript

[27] Any and all decisions of law made by this court may be appealed to the Supreme Court.
