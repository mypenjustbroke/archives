Interlocutory Appeal of Motion Decision in Domina et al. v SD [2025] SDCR 11

Date 4th November 2025
Judges
  • Judge Terak
  • Judge Brandmal
Held
  • The decision on the motion to dismiss is upheld. The case will continue.
Ruling 2–0
Applicable precedent
  • The definition of natural personhood in the Civil Code satisfies the test from In re Replacement of KingRed31 [2020] SDSC 21 and refined in Ppatpat and brandmal, ex parte thesigmasquad (Appellant) v State of SimDemocracy (Respondent) [2025] SDSC 13.
  • Said definition ought to include banned persons and persons only active on some territory but not another for that reason.
  • Harmless error (a correct decision supported by the wrong reasons) is no justification to overturn a decision in its entirety.

MAJORITY OPINION by Judge Brandmal

(with Judge Terak agreeing)

Introduction

[1] The petitioner appealed the decision of Judge creative to not dismiss a civil case before him, because the plaintiff was banned. Under Art. 2 of the Civil Code 2025:

”§1. A natural person is any member of r/SimDemocracy that isn’t a bot, that's recognized as having rights and obligations under the law from the moment they join r/SimDemocracy and its associated servers.”

[2] From this text, the petitioner interprets the plaintiff to have lost the status as a natural person when they were banned from the aforementioned subreddit. The respondent pointed to the trial Judge's argumentation and emphasized the potential of abuse by an authoritarian government if the Court were to follow this understanding of the section. Judge creative explained in his decision, that the section in question is silent on cessation of natural personhood, and that interpreting it strictly would violate the plaintiffs fundamental Rights. Lastly, the appellant in his filing also claims that In re Penalties and Tickets Act [2025] SDSC 17 [11] limits the right to a fair hearing to those properly brought before a court.

On natural personhood

[3] The Court disagrees with both the appellant and the respondent. A plain text reading of Art. 2 §1. of the Civil Code does indeed exclude those who are only members of the Discord from being natural persons. It does also exclude all people who are not permanently on the Reddit. As is obvious, this is not only an absurd result, but also one that can lead to some of those horror scenarios the trial Judge and the respondent painted out. Hence, this Court will apply the test established in In re Replacement of KingRed31 [2020] SDSC 21 and refined in Ppatpat and brandmal, ex parte thesigmasquad (Appellant) v State of SimDemocracy (Respondent) [2025] SDSC 13 to establish whether there is a need to go beyond a plain text reading.

[4] The question is therefore, whether the above is an undesired outcome, or to be precise, whether it is: “an undesirable outcome, where failure to do so [go beyond plain text reading] creates a material risk of harm to a user where a better written law (i.e. serving the original intent better) would have protected that user from that material risk of harm.”. The relevant section of the civil code is firstly undesirable, as Reddit membership is not required to become a citizen. Sticking with this reading however, created the material risk of harming all 'Discord-only' users, as they would be unable to participate in the economy side of SimDem. A better written law could have easily protected those users from that very harm, by simply including them, as is the intent of the civil code (enabling the economic activity of citizens as defined by the Constitution)

[5] Regarding banned persons, the same is true. The outcome is undesired, insofar as the legislative has made clear that even banned people have the capacity to seek redress for torts committed against them (or Art. 28 of the Civil Code is rendered useless). This reading also created a material harm, as those banned are essentially without rights, and the state could easily remove its creditors from the Discord to get rid of debt. (See Art. 23 of the Constitution.) A better written law could have prevented this harm, by either specifying a procedure for such cases or allowing all people that once joined the server to retain their Rights.

[6] This Court does not see itself as a legislator. We will therefore draw the most minimalist conclusion possible from the findings above that still prevent the described harm. Members of any territory of SimDem to which the Civil Code applies are natural persons in this sense, as made evident by the intent of the Civil Code. Note that personhood and citizenship as defined by the Constitution are not the same thing. Generally speaking, the differences between personhood, citizenship and entities are not really subject to a universal nomenclature in SimDem unfortunately.

[7] For banned people, the most minimalist answer is harder to find, which is why this Court will stick closely to the telos (rationale) of the Civil Code. The law aims to enable an economy and at the same time create a financial incentive to behave a certain way, both for the state and persons. Insodoing, it protects the Rights of those injured by another. This obligation to compensate another must extend to those, who have damaged another and then left the server.(See only Confused, ex parte Antimuggy (Appellant) v Notcommunist366 et al. (Respondent) [2025] SDSC 20) But when the Civil Code extends obligations to one person, it automatically connects those to rights. (See already Art. 2 §1. of the law) This Court then must do the same and extend rights to those not currently on the server, but who have been here before.

On standing

[8] What the Court has done now is not what Judge Creative did. He argued that the section of the Civil Code in question is silent on cessation of membership. While the Court disagrees with this interpretation from a plain text reading, it is a case of a harmless error, where the reasoning for the correct decision only ought to be supplanted. However, Judge creative also developed his own theory for standing, claiming: “After careful review of the laws and jurisprudence of SimDemocracy, this Court finds no norm expressly defining 'standing', the criteria required to bring a civil suit. In the absence of such provision, this Court defines standing as [...]”.

[9] While this Court commends the trial Judge for their ingenuity, said ingenuity is better situated in the Senate. The Court finds the paragraphs [26] to [31] of his verdict violate Article 10 §3. and §3.2. of the Constitution. The Civil Code defines the criteria to have rights under it, namely personhood and the definitions of Judge creative are not rooted in that law or any other precedent.

[10] Judges cannot establish a new test for limiting the access to Court and also define the new test without any such rooting, even if the parties wanted them to do so. To clarify, the Constitution, statutory law and precedent are the first tools of a judge before resorting to their best judgment alone.

[11] The aforementioned parts of the decision are overturned and the reasoning for it is supplanted by [3]-[7], but the decision as a whole is upheld.
