MAJORITY OPINION by Chief Justice TheLittleSparty

(with Justices IvyCactus, Syndicality, Ed, and Britz agreeing)

Introduction

[1] In response to the AI slop submitted by the petitioner, the actual text of the petition is almost entirely disregarded by this Court.

[2] In light of the significance of the question posed, that being whether or not the State has the ability to essentially renege on plea deals, this Court has elected to essentially render a summary verdict.

Summary of the Petition

[3] The petition consisted entirely of AI slop with the exception of the fundamental question posed.

Summary of the Response

[4] The respondent submitted several points in direct response to the AI slop submitted by the petitioner. Despite the nature of the response being directed towards a meaningless mess, there was one theme discussed several times which this Court sees fit to note.

[5] The respondent cites the Criminal Code, specifically Article 4 §3, which establishes terms under which a plea deal may be disregarded, and the respondent asserts that based on these terms, a plea deal is fundamentally not the same as a judicial decision. This assertion is repeated in the respondent’s notes regarding United States Supreme Court precedent.

The Ambit of the Petition

[6] The core question at the heart of an otherwise meaningless slop is thus:

[6.1] Can the State of SimDemocracy offer a plea deal to a defendant, then renege, modify, or otherwise refuse to uphold the terms of that plea deal at a later time?

[7] Where the respondent asserts that the duty of the Supreme Court of SimDemocracy is not to determine contractual breaches, it is in fact the duty of this Court to define what constitutes a contract, in particular with regards to the Constitution of SimDemocracy.

[8] Article 23, §1.3 of the Constitution reads: 

§1.3. The government cannot void any agreement with a citizen without providing equivalent appropriate compensation.

[9] In the case of a plea deal, the State of SimDemocracy [with the Attorney General representing the State, or government, as it were] is entering into a contractual agreement with a citizen [whom charges being brought against and subsequently a plea deal offered to intrinsically necessitates that said be a citizen] and thus is obligated constitutionally to offer “equivalent appropriate compensation”. As there can be no other compensation of a value equal to the freedom with which we are endowed by our Creator, the State fundamentally may not modify, change, cancel, or otherwise void the terms of a plea deal after it has been duly approved by a judge. If a higher court later determines the plea deal itself to have been fundamentally unsound in favor of the State, the defendant shall be freed. If a higher court later determines the plea deal itself to have been fundamentally unsound in favor of the defendant, it is the suggestion from this court that the Attorney General responsible be subjected to tarring and feathering in the streets.

Decision

[10] The petitioner is to be freed upon the completion of the original terms of their plea deal.

[11] In lieu of the inalienable right to liberty, the State is obligated to provide compensatory damages of no less than one (1) tau.
