MAJORITY OPINION by Judge Muggy

(With Chief Judge Tech Support and Judge Hmquestionable agreeing)

Introduction

[1] This case comes before the Court as a question of law from the Speaker of the Senate on whether the Vice President could tiebreak a vote despite, in the Speaker’s opinion, a Senator’s vote not counting because they didn’t hold their office upon the closing of the voting period.

[2] The Court used questioning in order to get a better understanding of the Speaker’s perspective, but otherwise did not hear argumentation.

Does a Senator’s vote count upon voting or upon a voting period’s completion?

[3] Given the fact that this is more of a question on technicalities, the Constitution doesn’t provide much insight on when exactly a Senator’s vote is counted.

[4] The sole section that may apply is §5.1 of Article 1, which reads:

“§5.1. A quorum shall be defined as a simple majority of the members of the relevant deliberative body of the Senate (whether the full Senate or any officially established subdivision) casting a vote or formally declaring their intention to abstain.”

[5] The most important part of this section being “the members […] casting a vote”, which backs the reading that a Senator’s vote is counted, at the very least for a quorum, upon casting the vote, which should extend to the actual substance of the vote, given that it’s what contributes to the quorum. We can hence extend this, more generally, to vote-counting.

Verdict

[6] When a Senator votes on any given motion or legislation, it counts upon voting, not upon the voting period coming to a close.

[7] The Vice President’s tiebreaking vote on the piece of legislation in question is valid.
