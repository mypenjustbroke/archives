In re Impeachment Petitions of Prime Minister Kelvin and Minister Sunbear [2021] SDSC 1

Date of judgment 24th January 2021
Justices
  • Chief Justice SeaOtter
  • Justice Syndicality
  • Justice Ivy Cactus
Held Impeachment of RightKelvin was lawful, Impeachment of Sunbear was not
Ruling 3-0
Applicable precedent
  • Possible consequences may affect the court’s remedies, but not base constitutionality of that being reviewed, [10.1]
    • Upholds In re 38th Presidential and 38th Senatorial Elections [2020] SDSC 10
  • The use of the word “Elections” under Section 1.1. Article 24 of the constitution only applies to general elections, [14]
  • Every position other than MP may be impeached under article 24, [16]

MAJORITY OPINION by Justice Ivy Cactus

(with Chief Justice Seaotter and Justice Syndicality agreeing.)

Intro

[1] The petitioner is seeking judicial review of the petitions to initiate impeachment against the Minister of Parliament Sunbear and the Prime Minister Rightkelvin (both from the position of which they were titled). They argue that the petitions violate Section 1.1. of Article 24 of the Constitution (Right to Confidence), which protects officials that are elected to represent a minority of Simdemocracy’s electorate from removal petitions.

Summary of Petition

[2] The petitioner first argues that because of Simdemocracy’s voting system, Sequential Proportional Score Voting, which is proportionally representational Members of Parliament do not represent a majority of Simdemocracy’s electorate, rather just their voting base. Therefore, they fall under the definition of Section 1.1., and may not be impeached through Article 24.

[3] They go on to explain that if this impeachment was allowed to go through, parties would be able to continually impeach Members of Parliament until they obtained a super majority.

[3.1] The petitioner provides an example of this happening in real life, that being when Mussolini impeached the opposition, effectively guaranteeing his dictatorship.

[4] The petitioner then slides to Rightkelvin’s impeachment. They argue that Parliament is not the majority of the electorate, it is instead representing Members of Parliament and their compromise, as seen through rainbow coalitions. Therefore, the Prime Minister represents a minority of the electorate, and can not be impeached by petition under Article 24.

[5] The petitioner then argues that the Supreme Court should think of the consequences of its actions. They speak of how allowing these impeachments would plunge SimDemocracy into populism, with wild impeachments. They argue this would erode our democracy, and cause SimDemocracy to go the way of the Weimar Republic.

[5.1] The petitioner notes that these arguments are not of a legal nature, but are important enough to be considered by the court.

Summary of Response

[6] The Respondent first argues that the Parliament represents the people of SimDemocracy as a whole, as is stated by Article 1 Section 1 of the Constitution (The Parliament shall be the primary legislative body of r/SimDemocracy and shall represent the people by wielding legislative authority), and therefore Members of Parliament represent the entirety of the electorate.

[6.1] This therefore means that the Prime Minister, who is elected by the Parliament, also represents SimDemocracy as a whole, and therefore needs to be held accountable to the people.

[7] The respondent then points out that the Prime Minister isn’t even elected, and therefore Section 1.1. doesn’t apply.

[8] They go on to make it clear that Members of Parliament are not the Majority of the electorate, and because the Prime Minister is elected by representatives they are not themselves a representative of the Majority of the electorate.

[9] The response concludes with a plea to the court to recognize the author’s intent, and that if Rightkelvin’s impeachment was invalidated only the President could be impeached through Article 24.

Consequences of the Courts Actions

[10] The court would first like to address the plethora of arguments made by both parties asking the court to look at the consequences of its actions, stating that we would cause grave problems if we decided one way or the other, with little connection to the legal problems. We would like to make it clear that these types of arguments are completely irrelevant, and the law doesn’t change because it is bad. It’s Parliament’s duty to change laws that have bad consequences.

[10.1] This, of course, does not mean that the Supreme Court doesn’t have to look at consequences when applying remedies, and it’s important for the court to do so. However, just because the Supreme Court may choose it’s remedy based on the possible outcomes, doesn’t mean the constitutionality of what the Supreme Court is reviewing changes. This is established under In re 38th Presidential and 38th Senatorial Elections [2020] SDSC 10 [35] when former Chief Justice Danyo chooses to address constitutionality and remedy separately.

[11] The Court pleads future petitioners and respondents to avoid pathos based arguments, they clutter arguments and are generally annoying to address (such as requiring an entire section such as this in an opinion).

Impeachment of Sunbear

[12] The Supreme Court agrees with the petitioner that Members of Parliament are in fact elected to represent their voter base and not SimDemocracy as a whole.

[12.1] While the court recognizes that our Constitution’s first section makes it clear Parliament represents all of SimDemocracy, Members of Parliament don’t. It’s the body as a whole coming together which makes Parliament successfully represent the electorate.

[13] Therefore, Members of Parliament do represent a minority of Simdemocracy’s electorate, and Section 1.1. of Article 24 applies.

[14] As a side note, we repeatedly saw the argument that this section is needed to hold Parliament accountable. This argument is entirely speculative and would not sway the court in any scenario, however it’s important for it to be known that elections and the Cabinet Secretary, as well as Parliament itself, are the bodies that hold Members of Parliament accountable.

Impeachment of Rightkelvin

[14] The court will start by interpreting Section 1.1., and its use of the word “election”, because it seems that this could be interpreted in two ways, to mean that any election from any individuals applies, or that only general elections apply. Due to the repeated use of “electorate”, which is defined to mean the number of people who have voted in the most recent Parliamentary election, the court thinks it's clear that only general elections, those being Presidential and Parliamentary elections, apply for the purposes of this section.

[14.1] This means that any elections carried out solely by Parliament do not apply, and those they elect are impeachable by petition.

[15] It should also be noted that Parliament as a whole does represent the entirety of SimDemocracy, so any decision they make through a majority vote is a vote which represents the majority of SimDemocracy’s electorate.

[16] This means that not only is the President impeachable through Article 24, as specified by the respondent, every position other than Member of Parliament is.

Conclusion

[17] Therefore, as Members of Parliament represent a minority of the electorate (that being their voting base), they fall under the exception given in Section 1.1. of the Constitution’s Article 24. This means that impeachment petitions against members of Parliament are unconstitutional.

[18] Due to the fact that only general elections apply for the purposes of Article 24, Section 1.1. of the Constitution, and the fact that the Prime Minister is elected by Parliament, a body which represents the majority of Simdemocracy’s population, the Prime Minister may be impeached by way of Article 24.

Verdict

[19] The impeachment petition invoked against Sunbear was unconstitutional, and therefore The Electoral Commission shall be barred from establishing a vote on the petition.

[20] The impeachment petition invoked against Rightkelvin was constitutional, and the injunction against the Electoral Commission is lifted. The petition shall be voted on immediately.
