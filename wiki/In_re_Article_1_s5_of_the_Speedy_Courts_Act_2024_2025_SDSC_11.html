In re Article 1 s5 of the Speedy Courts Act 2024 [2025] SDSC 11

Date of judgment 27th May 2025
Justices
  • Chief Justice TheLittleSparty
  • Justice Ivy Cactus
  • Justice Syndicality
Held Article 1, Section 5 of the Speedy Courts Act 2024 is unconstitutional
Ruling 3-0
Applicable precedent
  • The Supreme Court of SimDemocracy may issue precedential per curiam judgements, [3]
  • The Senate may not place procedural burdens on the Supreme Court, [4]

MAJORITY OPINION Per Curiam

(by a unanimous court)

Introduction

[1] The petitioner is seeking review into Article 1, Section 5 of the Speedy Courts Act 2024 for violating Article 12, Section 5 of the Constitution.

[2] This case was extremely straight-forward. The court, as such, is issuing this judgement summarily without argumentation by the petitioner and the state in response.

[3] This judgement marks a first in SimDemocracy history, a per curiam opinion. These judgements are reserved, generally, for summary judgements held without full argumentation. They represent a judgement not assigned to any one specific author, but to the court at-large, and are usually shorter and more simplistic than a full judgement. Such opinions are still, in this context, precedential and binding.

Considerations

[4] The Constitution, indeed, says that the Supreme Court has the ability to set its own procedures to meet its Constitutional and legal duties. While the legislature has the ability to give legal obligations to the Supreme Court, these legal obligations can not be for the Supreme Court to follow certain procedures when in relation to the court’s constitutional duties.

[5] The section in question places a time-limit on the Supreme Court’s deliberation and writing process, that being 14 days from adjournment of court. This is a procedural burden and therefore unconstitutional. It should be noted that the Supreme Court doesn't ever adjourn — the term isn’t particularly applicable for an inquisitorial court.

Verdict

[6] Article 1, Section 5 of the Speedy Courts Act is unconstitutional because it sets procedures for the Supreme Court.

[7] As this section applies to the inferior courts as well, wherein it is not unconstitutional, the court will give the Senate of SimDemocracy two (2) weeks to adjust it accordingly. After which, if the issues are not corrected, it will become null, void, and of no effect.
